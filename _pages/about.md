I am an [Associate Scientist](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/science/staff/jennifer-sobeck) at [Caltech/IPAC](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/) and the Lead Systems Scientist for the [Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/project/roman) Science Support Center (SSC) at IPAC. I oversee the Pipeline Development Team for the Microlensing Science Operations System (MSOS), the pipeline which will generate the higher-level data products for the Galactic Bulge Time Domain Survey (GBTDS), a Roman Core Community Survey. I work with teams at NASA Goddard and STScI to help prepare the mission for the data volumes of its wide-field surveys.

I am a stellar spectroscopist with a background in atomic and molecular physics. My research focuses on the chemical and kinematic characterization of stellar populations in the Milky Way and the Local Group.

Before joining IPAC, I was System Scientist for the Maunakea Spectroscopic Explorer at the Canada-France-Hawaii Telescope. Before that, I was SDSS-IV Project Manager for APOGEE-2 and Las Campanas Observatory operations, based at the University of Washington and, earlier, the University of Virginia. I received my Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Texas at Austin in 2007, then held postdoctoral positions at ESO, the University of Chicago (JINA; KICP affiliate), and the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur.

I am the primary developer of [MOOG_SCAT](https://github.com/jsobeck/MOOG_SCAT), a radiative transfer code that includes coherent, isotropic scattering.

## A Little About Me
My path into science began with films and books. *The Right Stuff* and, funnily enough, *SpaceCamp* sparked my fascination with space, and the books of Carl Sagan and Ann Druyan made me want to understand the universe. I am a wife and a mom to two young children, and I love to travel, especially to visit family in France and in Texas.
